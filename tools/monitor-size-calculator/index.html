This tool turns a monitor’s diagonal size and aspect ratio into its real physical dimensions - width and height in both inches and centimeters - plus the screen area and a plain-English desk-fit verdict. Enter a diagonal and pick an aspect ratio, or tap a preset for a common screen. It is the answer to “how wide is a 34-inch ultrawide, actually?” and “will this thing fit on my desk?”

The key insight the diagonal alone hides: width, not the diagonal, decides whether a screen fits your space, and two monitors with the same diagonal can be very different shapes. A 34-inch 21:9 ultrawide is 3.5 inches wider than a 32-inch 16:9, yet it is also shorter and has slightly less total screen area - because ultrawides trade height for width. A 49-inch 32:9 is nearly four feet (120 cm) across. The calculator makes those trade-offs concrete before you buy. One knock-on effect worth knowing if you are moving to an ultrawide: the wider field of view changes how a given in-game sensitivity feels, so it is worth re-checking your cm/360 in the mouse sensitivity converter after the swap.

Assumptions and sources

  • Width, height, and area are exact geometry from the diagonal and aspect ratio (height = diagonal / sqrt((w/h)^2 + 1)); there is no external data in the core result.
  • The 20-30 inch viewing-distance figure behind the desk-depth advice is consistent with OSHA’s computer-workstation guidance on monitor placement.
  • The bezel allowance (add roughly 0.5-1 inch to the calculated width) and the 8-10 inch stand-footprint figure are TechFuelHQ working assumptions from measuring common monitors, stated so you can check your exact model’s spec sheet when clearance is tight.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a monitor's width and height from the diagonal?
The diagonal and aspect ratio fix the physical width and height by simple geometry. For an aspect ratio of w:h, the height equals the diagonal divided by the square root of ((w/h) squared plus 1), and the width is that height times w/h. For example, a 27-inch 16:9 monitor is 23.5 inches (59.8 cm) wide and 13.2 inches (33.6 cm) tall, because 23.5 and 13.2 form a right triangle with a 27-inch hypotenuse. The calculator does this for any diagonal and aspect ratio and also gives you the screen area.
How wide is a 27-inch, 32-inch, 34-inch, or 49-inch monitor?
At 16:9: a 24-inch is about 20.9 inches (53 cm) wide, a 27-inch is 23.5 inches (60 cm), and a 32-inch is 27.9 inches (71 cm). Ultrawides are wider for the same diagonal because they are shorter: a 34-inch 21:9 is 31.4 inches (80 cm) wide but only 13.1 inches tall, and a 49-inch 32:9 super-ultrawide is a full 47.2 inches (120 cm) wide. The width is what decides whether a screen fits your desk, so two screens with the same diagonal can need very different amounts of room.
Will a 34-inch ultrawide fit my desk?
A 34-inch 21:9 ultrawide is about 31.4 inches (80 cm) wide at the panel, so it needs a desk at least roughly 100 cm (39 inches) wide to leave a little clearance, and more if you want speakers or a second device beside it. Depth is rarely the problem - the stand footprint is usually 8 to 10 inches deep - but a curved ultrawide sits slightly farther forward. If your desk is under about 100 cm wide, a 32-inch 16:9 (27.9 inches wide) or a monitor arm to push the screen back is the more comfortable fit.
Is a 34-inch ultrawide really bigger than a 32-inch 16:9?
Wider, but not taller, and only a little larger in total area. A 34-inch 21:9 is 31.4 inches wide versus 27.9 inches for a 32-inch 16:9, so it is about 3.5 inches wider. But the ultrawide is only 13.1 inches tall against the 32-inch’s 15.7 inches, so it is noticeably shorter. By screen area, the 34-inch ultrawide (412 square inches) is actually slightly smaller than the 32-inch 16:9 (438 square inches). Ultrawides trade height for width; if you want more vertical working space, the 16:9 wins.
Does the screen size include the bezel?
No. The advertised diagonal and every dimension this calculator gives are for the active display area, not the outer edge of the monitor. The physical chassis is larger by the bezel width - typically a quarter to half an inch per side on a modern monitor, more on older or budget panels. When you are checking desk fit or a multi-monitor layout, add roughly 0.5 to 1 inch (1 to 2.5 cm) total to the calculated width for the bezel and frame.
How much desk depth do I need for a monitor?
Less than people expect - the constraint is almost always width, not depth. A monitor stand’s footprint is usually 8 to 10 inches (20 to 25 cm) deep, and you want the screen 20 to 30 inches (50 to 75 cm) from your eyes, so a desk 24 to 30 inches deep is comfortable for almost any size. A monitor arm clamped to the back edge frees up that stand footprint and lets you push even a large ultrawide back to a proper viewing distance on a shallow desk.
