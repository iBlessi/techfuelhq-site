Enter your typical hours per day for each activity and your monthly download volume, set how many people share the connection, and you get a monthly GB total, a daily average, and how that compares to the common 1.2 TB US cap.

Assumptions and sources

  • The streaming rates (about 1 GB/hour SD, 3 GB/hour HD, 7 GB/hour 4K) are Netflix’s published data-usage figures; other services land near them but differ by codec and bitrate ladder.
  • YouTube rates are derived from YouTube’s recommended sustained speeds per resolution, converted from Mbps to GB/hour; actual usage varies with the specific video.
  • The 1.2 TB reference cap is the monthly threshold on Xfinity’s data usage plan; other providers set different caps or none, so substitute yours.
  • The video-call (1-2.5 GB/hour), music, gaming (~0.1 GB/hour of play), and browsing rates are TechFuelHQ working estimates chosen to sit mid-range of commonly observed values; they are stated here so you can swap in your own measured numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How much data does streaming video use?
By Netflix’s own published figures, standard-definition streaming uses about 1 GB per hour, HD (1080p) about 3 GB per hour, and 4K Ultra HD up to 7 GB per hour. YouTube runs a little higher: roughly 1.5 GB per hour at 1080p, and its recommended 20 Mbps sustained speed for 4K works out to about 9 GB per hour. Streaming is almost always the single largest item in a household’s data budget - one person watching 3 hours of HD a day is about 270 GB a month on its own, and the same hours in 4K is over 600 GB.
How much data does 4K streaming use per month?
At about 7 GB per hour, two hours of 4K a day is roughly 420 GB a month for one person; four hours a day is about 840 GB. Add a second 4K viewer in the house and you are knocking on the door of a 1.2 TB monthly cap from streaming alone. If you are regularly streaming 4K on multiple screens, an unlimited plan usually costs less than the overage fees on a capped plan.
Will I exceed a 1.2 TB data cap?
The 1.2 TB (1,229 GB) cap used by Comcast/Xfinity and some other US providers sounds huge, but heavy 4K streaming households reach it. As a rule of thumb: light use (browsing, SD/HD streaming, some gaming) lands well under 500 GB a month; a single 4K-heavy streamer or a gaming household that downloads a few big titles runs 600-1,000 GB; two or more 4K screens plus large game downloads can pass 1.2 TB. Enter your real habits above to see where you land.
How much data does online gaming use?
Actual gameplay is light - roughly 40 to 150 MB per hour for most online games (call it 0.1 GB per hour), because the game only sends position and state updates, not video. What eats data is downloads and updates: a modern AAA title is often 50-150 GB to install, and live-service games push multi-gigabyte patches regularly. So gaming’s data footprint is mostly the downloads line, not the hours-played line.
How much data do video calls use?
A one-on-one HD video call (Zoom, Meet, Teams) uses roughly 1 to 1.5 GB per hour; group HD calls can run higher, up to about 2.5 GB per hour. Audio-only calls are a fraction of that, around 30-60 MB per hour. For a full-time remote worker on HD calls several hours a day, that is a meaningful 30-90 GB a month.
How can I reduce my internet data usage?
The biggest lever is streaming resolution: dropping Netflix or YouTube from 4K to 1080p cuts that activity’s data by more than half. Cap streaming quality in each app’s settings, download podcasts and music for offline use, schedule large game downloads when you are not near a cap limit, and use audio-only mode for long calls. If you consistently exceed your cap, compare the overage fees against your provider’s unlimited add-on - unlimited is often cheaper than two months of overages.
