Quick answer

Day one of TechFuelHQ’s 8TB hard-drive price index: of 673 listings reviewed across 12 tracked models on 2026-08-12, exactly one passed every rule — WD Red Pro 8TB at Best Buy for $402.99, or $50.37 per terabyte. Marketplace sellers, multi-packs, recertified drives, and capacity-ambiguous titles are rejected, not guessed at. Open data, CC BY 4.0.

By LK Wood IV · Published 2026-08-12 · ~5 min read · St. Louis County, MO

The short version

Consumer NAS drive prices rose roughly 30-50% through the 2026 supply squeeze as WD and Seagate steered nearline output toward AI and cloud buyers, and most of the $/TB numbers that circulate are undated screenshots or grey-market recertified listings quoted as if they were new-drive prices. This page starts a dated, checkable record: a $/TB index for 8TB 3.5-inch internal drives — the mainstream homelab size — observed at named direct US retailers only, with the method, the acceptance rules, every rejection reason, and the raw data published alongside the numbers.

It is labeled a pilot because the index metric has an activation gate it has not passed yet. Day one produced exactly one qualifying observation, and rather than loosen the rules to manufacture a fuller table, the gate status is printed here verbatim.

What gets rejected, and why that’s the point

672 of 673 reviewed listings were rejected, each with recorded machine-readable reasons (a listing can carry several): 583 came from sellers off the direct-retailer allowlist — eBay storefronts, recertified-drive houses, white-label resellers, marketplace suffixes; 202 never stated the 8TB capacity in the title; 130 carried prices outside the sanity band; 55 were multi-packs or bundles; 52 named a sibling line (IronWolf Pro under the IronWolf query, SkyHawk AI under SkyHawk); 33 were external or enclosure products; 5 were used, recertified, or open-box.

The capacity bucket is the HDD-specific finding: direct retailers frequently publish family-level Google Shopping titles — real examples from this capture include “Gold Hard Drive WD” and “Exos 7E10 Hard Drive Seagate” at $399.99 — that never say which capacity the price belongs to. A tracker that guessed would fill its table faster and be worthless. The rejection log ships with the dataset.

Method, exactly

Captures run against Google Shopping (US, English) via DataForSEO’s merchant API, one exact-model query per tracked drive. An offer qualifies only if the seller matches a named direct-retailer allowlist (Best Buy, Newegg, B&H, Micro Center, Adorama, Walmart first-party, and peers — marketplace suffixes excluded), the title states the exact line and the 8TB capacity, no sibling-superset line token appears, the product is a single internal drive (external/enclosure listings and multi-pack markers like “2-Pack”, “2 x”, or “20PK” are rejected), condition is new (used, refurbished, recertified, and white-label all rejected), and the USD price sits inside a $100-600 band.

Two method corrections were made against measured evidence on day one, both recorded in the change log below: the price ceiling was raised from $400 after a real single-drive offer — the WD Red Pro at $402.99 — died on the old band as its only rejection reason, and a multi-pack guard was added in the same pass because pack listings ($599.98 to $1,479.96 for 2- and 4-packs in the same capture) pass every title rule and would otherwise have entered the raised band as fake singles.

Amazon is excluded by design (see FAQ). The full collector, product basket, tests, raw responses, and rejection logs are in the site’s repository.

Frequently asked questions

What does an 8TB NAS hard drive cost right now?
In this dataset’s day-one capture (2026-08-12), the only 8TB drive that passed every rule — named direct US retailer, new condition, explicit 8TB capacity and line name in the listing title, single drive — was WD Red Pro 8TB at Best Buy for $402.99, about $50.37 per terabyte. Grey-market recertified 8TB drives list far lower, but they are a different product and are excluded by design.
Why are Amazon listings not counted in this index?
Amazon Associates policy allows Amazon price data to be shown only through Amazon’s own live mechanisms, so a dated, downloadable snapshot of Amazon prices is not something this site can publish — the same compliance line that forced price columns out of the PC parts dataset on 2026-08-01. Amazon listings also blend marketplace sellers with first-party stock in ways a mechanical seller rule cannot cleanly separate, and this index only publishes observations it can defend row by row.
Why does day one show only 1 of 12 tracked drives?
Two honest bottlenecks. First, most 8TB listings on Google Shopping come from grey-market sellers (recertified, white-label, eBay storefronts), which this index rejects on principle. Second, direct retailers often publish family-level titles like ‘Exos 7E10 Hard Drive Seagate’ with no capacity stated — that $399.99 listing could be the 8TB or the 10TB, so the collector rejects it as ambiguous rather than guessing. The index metric activates once 6 of 12 tracked drives produce qualifying offers on the same day.
How often is this captured, and what can I reuse?
The collector is built for daily capture, one row per UTC day once the activation gate opens. Every capture stores the complete raw API response, every accepted offer, and every rejected offer with machine-readable reasons. The per-offer observations file is CC BY 4.0 — reuse it with attribution to TechFuelHQ linking back to this page.
