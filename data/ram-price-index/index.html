Quick answer

This index tracks 32GB (2x16GB) DDR5 kits at named direct US retailers, with Amazon excluded by design. In the day-one capture on 2026-08-12, the cheapest DDR5-6000 kit passing every verification rule was $449.99 at B&H — about $14.06 per GB, roughly 4x its 2024 street price. Open CC BY 4.0 data.

By LK Wood IV · Published 2026-08-12 · ~5 min read · St. Louis County, MO

The short version

DDR5 prices roughly tripled during the 2026 memory supply crisis, and the numbers that circulate are mostly screenshots without dates or methods. This page is the start of a dated, checkable record: a $/GB index for mainstream 32GB (2x16GB) DDR5 kits observed at named direct US retailers only, with the method, the acceptance rules, every rejection reason, and the raw data published alongside the numbers.

It is labeled a pilot because the index metric has an activation gate it has not passed yet — and rather than loosen the rules to manufacture a number, the gate status is printed here verbatim.

What gets rejected, and why that’s the point

Of 546 listings reviewed in the day-one capture, 525 were rejected, each with recorded reasons: marketplace and third-party sellers (the largest bucket), listings that don’t state the 32GB capacity or the 2x16GB split in the title, wrong speed for the tracked SKU, used/renewed condition, and prices outside a sanity band. A price index built on listings a script guessed about would be smoother — and worthless. The rejection log ships with the dataset.

Method, exactly

Captures run against Google Shopping (US, English) via DataForSEO’s merchant API, one exact-model query per tracked kit. An offer qualifies only if the seller matches a named direct-retailer allowlist (Best Buy, Newegg, B&H, Micro Center, Adorama, Walmart first-party, and peers — marketplace suffixes excluded), the title states the exact kit line, speed, 32GB capacity, and 2x16GB split (a “Dual Channel” declaration on a 32GB listing counts as the split), condition is new, and the USD price sits inside a $60–900 band. The band’s ceiling was raised from $500 on day one when real direct-retailer offers at $469–499 for the cheapest kit proved the old ceiling was written for a market that no longer exists — that correction is recorded in the change log, not hidden.

Amazon is excluded by design (see FAQ). The full collector, product basket, tests, raw responses, and rejection logs are in the site’s repository.

Frequently asked questions

What does 32GB of DDR5 cost right now?
In this dataset’s day-one capture (2026-08-12), the cheapest 32GB (2x16GB) DDR5-6000 kit that passed every rule — named direct US retailer, new condition, explicit kit configuration in the listing title — was $449.99 (about $14.06/GB) for Corsair Vengeance at B&H. Corsair’s Dominator Titanium 6000 kit was $649.99 at Newegg. Marketplace and third-party-seller listings, which are sometimes cheaper and often less reliable, are excluded by design.
Why is Amazon excluded from this index?
Two reasons. Amazon Associates policy only permits displaying Amazon price data through Amazon’s own live mechanisms, so a dated open snapshot of Amazon prices cannot be published — the same policy that led this site to remove prices from its PC parts dataset on 2026-08-01. And Amazon listings mix marketplace sellers with first-party stock in ways a mechanical rule cannot reliably separate. Direct retailers with named storefronts make each observation checkable.
Why does the index show only 3 of 12 kits today?
Because the acceptance rules are strict and public: the listing must come from a named direct retailer, state the 32GB capacity and the 2x16GB module split explicitly, name the exact kit line and speed, be new condition, and carry a plausible USD price. In the day-one capture only Corsair’s lines produced qualifying direct-retailer offers; G.Skill, Kingston, TeamGroup, and Crucial 2x16 kits surfaced mostly through marketplace sellers or ambiguous listings, which are rejected rather than guessed at. The index metric activates at 6 qualifying kits.
How often is this captured?
The collector is built for daily capture, one row per UTC day once the activation gate opens. Every capture stores its complete raw API response, every accepted offer, and every rejected offer with the exact reasons, so any published number can be traced to the listing it came from.
