Quick answer

The RX 9070 XT is about 7–12% faster than the RX 9070 at 1440p and pulls further ahead in ray tracing, but it draws 84W more (304W vs 220W). Both share 16GB of GDDR6 and the same die. Buy the XT for max out-of-box frames; buy the 9070 for efficiency and value.

TL;DR · The verdict in 2026

  • Performance: the XT is ~7–12% faster at 1440p (~9% at 4K, up to ~16% in ray tracing) — real, but smaller than the name implies.
  • The catch is power: the XT draws 84W more (304W vs 220W board power) for that ~10%, so it runs hotter and wants a bigger PSU.

The 9070 family’s confusing middle

AMD’s RDNA 4 line put three cards within a hair of each other — the RX 9070, the RX 9070 XT, and the 12GB 9070 GRE — and the two that matter most for a mainstream build are the first two. They look nearly identical on a spec sheet, share the same Navi 48 GPU and the same 16GB of memory, and sell within about a hundred dollars of each other. So the real question isn’t “which is better” — the XT is, plainly — it’s “is the XT’s extra performance worth its extra power and price for you?” That’s a narrower, more answerable question, and the numbers settle it. (If you’re cross-shopping the plain 9070 against NVIDIA instead, we cover that in RTX 5070 vs RX 9070.)

Both cards are cut from the same silicon. The XT simply has more of it switched on, running faster:

The XT enables about 14% more compute units and stream processors and clocks higher out of the box — on paper a decent jump. But paper isn’t frames, and the same die plus identical memory means the two behave far more alike than, say, a 5070 versus a 5070 Ti (which differ in VRAM too). How much faster is the XT, really?

Independent testing lands in a tight, consistent band. GamersNexus measured the 9070 XT “roughly 9–12% better than the 9070 in most of the games we tested” at 1440p — for example Dragon’s Dogma 2 at 116 FPS (XT) vs 106 FPS (9070), a 9.3% gap. PCWorld put the average lower still, at about 7% across their suite. So the honest figure is roughly 7–12% at 1440p — GamersNexus saw a similar ~9% at 4K (Dragon’s Dogma 2 at 70 vs 64 FPS) and a wider gap in ray tracing, up to about 16%, where the XT’s extra compute stretches its legs.

That’s a real, repeatable edge — but it’s the kind of gap you measure with a frame counter, not one you feel across a session. Both cards clear high-refresh 1440p comfortably and handle entry 4K. Drivers have also matured since these March-2025 launch reviews, nudging RDNA 4 up a little across the board, but the relative order hasn’t changed.

The catch: 84 watts for ~10%

Here’s the trade the spec sheet makes plain. That ~7–12% comes at a 304W board power rating versus 220W — an 84W jump — and independent testing measured real draw around 310W (XT) versus 224W (9070). GamersNexus called the plain 9070 “incredibly efficient” and described it as sitting in “the so-called sweet spot of power and performance,” delivering more frames per watt than its bigger sibling.

In a real build that gap matters more than a single-digit FPS delta. The 9070 runs cooler and quieter, fits comfortably on a quality 550–650W supply, and puts less heat into a small case; the XT wants stronger cooling, better airflow, and headroom on the PSU. Size either one with our PSU wattage calculator before you buy — the XT’s draw is a real line item, not a rounding error.

The tiebreaker that isn’t: VRAM

One of the biggest levers in most GPU comparisons is simply off the table here. Both cards carry 16GB of GDDR6 on a 256-bit bus — identical memory, identical 640 GB/s of bandwidth. Neither will run out of VRAM before the other, and neither ages better on that axis. That’s the opposite of a cross-brand fight like RTX 5070 vs RX 9070, where the 12GB-vs-16GB question does real work. Between these two Radeons, forget VRAM and decide on performance, power, and price alone.

Undervolt and the value case

RDNA 4 responds unusually well to tuning, and it cuts both ways in this matchup. The RX 9070 XT is a popular undervolt target — dropping voltage trims its considerable power draw and temperatures with little performance loss (and, because the card is power-limited, often adds a few frames), taking the sting out of that 304W rating. But the same flexibility means the base RX 9070 overclocks well, so a tuned 9070 can close much of the gap to a stock XT. That’s why the community verdict — echoed in the buying advice that shows up right in Google’s own summary for this matchup — is that a cheaper 9070 you push a little is often the smarter money than paying up for the XT and leaving it stock.

The price picture in 2026

At MSRP this was an easy call: $549 vs $599 is a $50 (9%) step for ~7–12% more performance, so the XT was a textbook upsell. But like the rest of the market in 2026, neither sells at MSRP. In a live Amazon check on 2026-07-20:

  • RX 9070 cards ran roughly $610–686 (an XFX Swift near $620, a PowerColor Reaper toward $686).
  • RX 9070 XT cards ran roughly $690–750 for mainstream models (premium designs past $800).

So the street premium has widened to about $80–100, not $50 — enough that the 9070’s efficiency and lower price tilt the value math back toward it. The XT is still the right buy if you want the most frames out of the box or you game at 4K; the 9070 is the pick if the premium is real to you or efficiency matters. A GDDR shortage keeps these prices volatile, so treat the snapshot as a method and check the live price before you commit.

So which should you buy?

  • Want the most performance out of the box, or game at 4K: the RX 9070 XT. You pay ~$80–100 more and 84W more for 7–12% higher frames, more in ray tracing ($690, PowerColor Reaper RX 9070 XT, Amazon).
  • Value efficiency, a quiet build, or a smaller PSU: the RX 9070. Same 16GB, 90% of the frames, 84W less heat, and it undervolts-or-overclocks to taste ($620, XFX Swift RX 9070, Amazon).
  • Cross-shopping NVIDIA: see how the tier lines up in RTX 5070 Ti vs RX 9070 XT and RTX 5070 vs RX 9070.

The one thing not to do is buy the XT purely because it has the “XT” on the box. It’s the faster card, but the gap is single-digits-to-low-double-digits, the power cost is real, and at 2026 street prices the plain 9070 is often the smarter buy. Decide on the price gap in front of you, not the badge.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a big difference between the RX 9070 and RX 9070 XT?
Smaller than the naming suggests. Independent testing (GamersNexus, PCWorld) puts the RX 9070 XT roughly 7–12% ahead of the RX 9070 at 1440p rasterization, with the gap widening to as much as ~16% in ray tracing. Both cards use the same Navi 48 die, the same 16GB of GDDR6, and the same 256-bit bus — the XT simply enables more compute units (64 vs 56) and clocks higher, which is also why it draws far more power.
Is the RX 9070 XT worth it over the RX 9070?
It depends on the price gap. At their $549 vs $599 MSRPs the XT was only $50 more for ~7–12% more performance — an easy upsell. At July 2026 street prices the premium is wider (roughly $80–100), and the plain 9070 draws 84W less while keeping the same 16GB of VRAM. If you want maximum out-of-the-box frames or game at 4K, pay for the XT; if you value efficiency, a cooler and quieter build, or a smaller PSU, the 9070 is the better buy.
Is the RX 9070 XT considered high end?
It sits at the top of the upper-midrange tier, not the true high end. AMD positions it against the RTX 5070 Ti, and it trades blows with that card in rasterization while trailing in heavy ray tracing. Above it in AMD’s own stack there is currently no larger RDNA 4 desktop card, but NVIDIA’s RTX 5080 and RTX 5090 sit in a higher performance-and-price class entirely. For 1440p and entry 4K gaming the 9070 XT is a strong, current card.
How much more power does the RX 9070 XT use than the RX 9070?
A lot. The RX 9070 XT carries a 304W board power rating versus 220W for the RX 9070 — an 84W difference — and independent testing measured real draw around 310W versus 224W. That extra power is the cost of the XT’s higher clocks and enabled compute units, and it means the XT wants a beefier cooler, better case airflow, and a higher-wattage PSU. You can size your supply for either card with our PSU wattage calculator.
Can you undervolt the RX 9070 XT to run cooler?
Yes, and it is one of the most popular tweaks for the card. RDNA 4 responds well to undervolting: many owners drop voltage to cut power draw and temperatures by a meaningful margin with little or no performance loss. The flip side is that the base RX 9070 also overclocks well, which is why the community often says a tuned 9070 can close much of the gap to a stock 9070 XT — making the value case for the cheaper card even stronger.
Do the RX 9070 and RX 9070 XT have the same VRAM?
Yes — both carry 16GB of GDDR6 on a 256-bit bus, delivering the same 640 GB/s of bandwidth. That is an important point in this matchup: unlike cross-brand comparisons where VRAM capacity can be a tiebreaker, the two Radeons are identical on memory, so neither ages better than the other on that front. The only differences are compute performance, power draw, and price.
